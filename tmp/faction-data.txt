General
Name:
Dreadbringers
Founding Chapter:
Dark Angels
Founding:
Second Founding
Specialty:
Massacres, purges, and total annihilation of the enemy




Homeworld
Name:
Advex-Mors Primus
Type:
Feudal World
Relation:
Stewards and Protectors
Population:
~150 million
Location:
Advex-Mors system, Tamahl Sector, Segmentum Obscurus (Currently Imperium Nihilus)
Description:
Advex-Mors Primus is a feudal world located in the Advex-Mors system, the location of the First Rangdan Xenocide during the great crusade. Upon the conclusion of the xenocide, the first legion set up an outpost on the world of the largest battle of the xenocides to keep eternal watch for the possible return of the Rangda. This outpost was manned by the newly created Order of the Broken Claws, the final survivors of which would form some of the founding members of the Dreadbringers Chapter. It was for this reason that Advex-Mors Primus was chosen for the Chapter's homeworld as it had already been the order's home since the end of the first Rangdan xenocide decades earlier.
Advex-Mors Primus was settled after the scouring and is now classified as a feudal world, trapped in the northernmost reaches of Imperium Nihilus. The planet itself is largely covered in a mix of wide plains, forrests, lakes, and highlands.




Fortress Monastery
Name:
Lastwatch Hold
Type:
Fortified castle/complex
Location:
Spread over multiple peaks in a region of fells and tarns in the northern hemisphere, relatively far from any centres of civilization on the planet, though not completely unreachable.
Description:
Appearing to almost meld into and augment the environment it's built into, Lastwatch Hold is spread over multiple peaks and valleys. The higher reaches and towers augment the hills below to look like a mountain range made miniature, while the ground levels of the fortress appear to be a natural part of the environment. In the deepest reaches of the fortress can be found the original chantry of the Order of the Broken Claws established by the First Legion during the Great Crusade, inside which is kept stasis vaults containing relics and weapons captured from vanquished foes. This task, originally taken up by the Order of Broken Claws, is now the responsibility of the Chapter.
Visually Lastwatch Hold was modelled after the castles and keeps of the old knightly orders of Caliban, with thick walls and towers topped by crenellations.

Chapter Founding
The Dreadbringers were created during the Second Founding, and as such are one of the oldest space marine successor chapters.
The forces that made up the first Dreadbringers were taken from 3 main groups within the Dark Angels Legion, those being the Order of the Broken Claws, the Dark Angels 51st Chapter(the Sable Griffons), and the Twenty-Third Order.
The Order of the Broken claws was one of the First Legion’s Orders of the Hekatonystika. Established at the conclusion of the Second Rangdan Xenocide, the Order of the Broken Claws held the only full and complete account of the conflict as well as information on the Rangda Xenos. The Order’s warriors were trained to stand against the worst Rangdan bio-forms in combat, and during the bloody Third Rangdan Xenocide won much renown combating the Rangdan warmasters. After the war, and with the alleged annihilation of the Xenos, the Order dwindled in number, however they were not disbanded, instead continuing to guard a chantry on Advex-Mors Primus where the Rangda had first been encountered by the Imperium.
The Sable Griffons was the name of the 51st Chapter of the Dark Angels legion. A garrison of the chapter was present in the Advex-Mors system alongside the Order of the Broken Claws when the Sons of Horus Heavy Cruiser Conquerer’s Pride, fresh from their traitorous actions at Istvaan III, arrived in the system and lead an attack on the outpost, seeking relics and weapons of the Rangda to use in the Heresy.
Clearly underestimating the defenders skill and ferocity, the Sons of Horus were forced to flee with a fraction of what they had come for when, despite outnumbering the Dark Angels defenders, they were unable to fully break through before reinforcements could arrive to aid the knights of the Order. This battle was the first recorded engagement of the Dark Angels in the Heresy, and would forge a bond between the warriors of the Broken Claws and Sable Griffons. A bond forged in blood and battle. It was because of this close bond that the two groups together became the core of the newly christened Dreadbringers Chapter.
Due to this, and the choice to have the outpost on Advex-Mors Primus as the Chapter’s Fortress Monastery, the Order of the Broken Claws greatly influenced the culture of the Chapter.
The third group to form the first Dreadbringers was the Twenty-Third Order, though not much information about this Order remains. What is known is that upon the creation of the Dreadbringers, the Twenty-Third Order brought with it its Lord and Commander Marduk Sedras, the first Dreadmaster (Chapter Master) of the Dreadbringers
Marduk Sedras
Before the Second Founding, Marduk Sedras was an Eskaton of the Dreadwing. A veteran of the earliest battles of the Unification Wars, he had seen battle on countless worlds alongside Primarchs and the Emperor himself. His experience served to inspire and educate new recruits. He originally belonged to the Host of Stone, but later entered the ranks of the Dreadwing. Rather than join the Council of Masters on Gramarye, Sedras opted to accept a lesser rank of Master so that he could remain in the field.
At the start of the Heresy, Sedras was one of the oldest living Space Marines, having faithfully served the Emperor for more than 250 years. There was very little that he had not seen, so Sedras could impart his wisdom to nearby Dark Angels and inspire them to even greater deeds. During the Thramas Crusade Sedras commanded the Dreadwing in several battles and during the last stage of the Crusade launched a Phosphex purge of Thramas to prevent it from falling into the hands of the Night Lords. After landing on the planet, Sedras slew the surviving Night Lords commander Nakrid Thole.
        When Farith Redloss took the role of Supreme Grand Master of the Dark Angels, Sedras was then the highest ranking, longest serving, and most respected member of the Dreadwing. When the time of the Second Founding came and the First Legion was to be split into self-sustaining Chapters, Marduk Sedras volunteered to lead one of these new Chapters. His experience in leading and commanding Space Marines, especially those of the Dreadwing, was irrefutable, and he recognised that these Chapters would need people with his kind of experience at their head if they were to be effective.
        Due to his experience and standing in the Dreadwing, Sedras was given the honour kf being allowed to take their name and heraldry for his new Chapter. While he chose to use the emblem of the Dreadwing as the new Chapter’s sigil, however he modified the name. The wings of the Hexagrammaton were designed to work in harmony with each other to form a fighting force greater than the sum of its parts, and a self-sustaining Chapter needed to be that whole. As such, he modified the name, and dubbed his new Chapter the “Dreadbringers”.
        Marduk Sedras would eventually fall in battle. When he did his relic suit of Cataphractii-pattern Terminator Armour known as the Regalia of the Shattered Sceptre, as well as his great sword Death of Worlds, passed to his successor as Dreadmaster and would become de facto badges of office, being inherited by each Dreadmaster since.


Chapter Culture
The Dreadbringers maintain much of the culture from the First Legion, and in particular the old Dreadwing of the Hexagrammatron. Because of this they use names and terms totally unique to them. These unique terms are:
* Dreadmaster - Chapter Master
* Naufragia - Terminator Armoured 1st Company
* Broken Claws - 2nd Company
* Eskaton - A warrior that has overseen the final death of an entire race or world


Relation to humans
General population:
While individual humans should expect little in the way of courtesy from the Dreadbringers, they see themselves as protectors of the species. To be humanity's sword and shield is a great honour that each and every Dreadbringer takes very seriously.
Psykers:
Psykers can be both powerful allies/assets, and a dangerous threat, a truth known well to the Dreadbringers. The Chapter's Librarians are held with great respect, as are those of other highly disciplined Chapters such as other Unforgiven, Imperial Fists, Red Scorpions, etc. Sanctioned psykers of the Imperium are recognised for their power, but due to their less capable minds and bodies are seen as volatile assets not to be relied upon.
Abhumans:
Abhumans are treated no differently to any other human, as long as the abhuman does their duty to humanity and the Emperor.
Serfs:
Serfs are recognised as official members of the Chapter and are treated with the respect that recognition brings. The Chapter could not perform their duties and fulfil its purpose without the work of the serfs, a fact all Dreadbringers recognise.
Serf culture:
Serfs are organised into a rigid command structure, allowing them to carry out their non-combat duties with efficiency. The marines of the Chapter are of course regarded with great respect and reverence, but the serfs are well aware that they could not carry out their righteous campaigns without serf assistance.
Equerries:
While the command staff of the Chapter have equerries due to their expanded duties and responsibilities, individual battle-brothers do not. Instead each squad will have a team of serfs assigned to them that serve the same role.




Relations with Imperial organisations
Adeptus Mechanicus:
Relations with the Tech Priests do not lean strongly either positively or negatively, however, the Chapter refrains from showing off some of the more esoteric archeotech in their possession. Like other unforgiven Chapters, Techmarines are never made members of the Inner Circle, for their oaths to the Adeptus Mechanicus make their loyalty naturally suspect.
Adeptus Astartes Chapters:
As one of the Unforgiven, the Dreadbringers maintain extremely close ties with all Chapters carrying the geneseed of Lion El'Jonson. As a second-founding Chapter the Dreadbringers hold high standing within the Unforgiven. Of special note, the Dreadbringers are very close to and work regularly with the Consecrators Chapter as both groups aim to preserve and maintain the values, history, and traditions of the first legion.
Outside of the unforgiven, relations are mixed. A culture clash exists with any Chapter that puts a focus on seeking glory and grand honours such as the Ultramarines and Space Wolves. While not disregarding the usefulness of the Codex Astartes completely, Chapters that refuse to act outside of its words are seen as inefficient, ineffective, and incompetent by the Dreadbringers.
Astra Militarum:
As humans prepared to lay down their lives in service of the Imperium, the members of the guard receive great respect from the Dreadbringers. When participating in a campaign or battle alongside the Astra Militarum, the commander of the guard forces is invited to the command table as they are recognised for having the best knowledge of their forces, and will value their views and opinions. Despite this respect however, the Dreadbringer’s commanders will not hesitate to overrule their guard counterparts when necessary. The Dreadbringers are ready to lay down the lives of every guardsman in the name of victory, as they are ready to do with their own lives.
Adeptus Astra Telepathica:
Astropaths are recognised for their vital role within the imperium and are respected as such. While the Chapter does make use of astropaths, it also ensures that its own Librarius is capable of filling the role if the need arose.
Ecclesiarchy:
The Dreadbringers do not revere the Emperor as a god, and do not follow the “Imperial Creed”, instead believing in the Imperial Truth of the Great Crusade. As such they regard the Ecclesiarchy with contempt. This rarely causes problems for either side however. The Dreadbringers have learned to hold their tongues where necessary, and as a rather insular Chapter (as most Unforgiven Chapters are) the Ecclesiarchy has not found the need to take any action. They are likely not even aware the theological difference between them exists.
Inquisition:
While the inquisition is recognised for its usefulness at investigating the general populace of the Imperium, they overstep their mark. The Dreadbringers refuse to accept or follow orders given to them by the inquisition, instead focussing on their own plans and goals. This has brought them many run-ins with overzealous inquisitors looking to beat the stubborn Chapter into line. Because of their specialty in using devastating and exterminatus weaponry, the Dreadbringers have had encounters with the Ordo Exterminatus.
As one of the Unforgiven, the Dreadbringers also carry the suspicions that all members of this group do due to their continuing close-ties to each other and accusations of legion-building. The inquisition abhors secrets being kept from them, and the unforgiven guard theirs with their lives.
Deathwatch:
The Dreadbringers regularly send marines to the Deathwatch, being among the first Chapters to swear an oath to tithe warriors to the Deathwatch. Due to their origins from the Order of the Broken Claws who's charge was to stand vigilant for a returning xenos threat, the purpose and aims of the Deathwatch are well understood and recognised by the Dreadbringers, and to have served in the Deathwatch is seen as an honour.
Rogue Traders:
With Rogue Traders being so varied, there is no overarching opinion that the Chapter has of them. If they serve the Emperor and the Imperium then they shall receive the same respect as any imperial who does the same. Rogue Traders that aim to discover new worlds and human civilisations to claim them in the name of the Emperor are seen to be continuing the work of the Great Crusade, and may receive aid from the Dreadbringers, whereas those that try to exercise control or authority over any member of the Chapter will be ignored and treated with disdain, no matter the words of the trader's warrant of trade.
Adepta Sororitas:
When fighting together the Dreadbringers and the Adepta Sororitas have proven to be an effective team, especially on missions where purges and eradication are the goal. They are recognised as the effective and capable force that they are, however their extreme displays of religious faith and reverence of the "God Emperor" means the Sisters of Battle are uncomfortable allies. When their faith results in inefficiencies in their actions, the Dreadbringers views can range from mere disappointment, to outright contempt.




Hated foes
Chaos:
All servants and followers of Chaos are reviled by the Dreadbringers, but a particular grudge has developed with the Thousand Sons warband the “Scions of Erudition''. There is no particular reason other than familiarity; the two forces have faced each other more often than would be expected. The Dreadbringers have been scouring their archives, with the help of thr Consecrators chapter, for evidence relating to the rumour that that Lord of the Scions of Erudition, Orasmyn, was once seconded to the Dark Angels legion during the Great Crusade.
The Dreadbringers, due to their propensity for devastator squads and total destruction, are especially effective against the Rubricae of the Thousand Sons as if their armour is completely destroyed, it can take decades if not longer to be reformed; a massive blow for the sons of the Crimson King.
Xenos:
While the Chapter is ever vigilant for any return of the Rangda due to their origins from the Order of Broken Claws, the xenos species has not been seen since the xenocides of the Great Crusade. However during the xenocides, the Slaugth xenos usually followed in the rangda’s wake. The Slaugth were not totally eliminated during the Great Crusade, and as such have plagued the northern Imperium on a number of occasions, though never on a massive scale. This is partly thanks to the work of the Dreadbringers, who are likely the most experienced force in the galaxy at combatting the Slaugth.
Of the more familiar xenos species to the Imperium, the Dreadbringers have had notable run-ins with Necrons of the Sautekh Dynasty, Harlequins of an unspecified troupe, and a clan of Goff Orks of unspecified allegiance.


Recruitment
Location(s) of recruitment:
The vast majority of aspirants are recruited from the peoples of Advex-Mors Primus and Advex-Mors system as a whole, though promising members of any world can be selected by the Chapter. These worlds tend to be feudal worlds.
Aspriant selection and trials:
When the Dreadbringers come to a world for recruitment, people from all over the planet converge to put their sons up for selection. Events and tournaments are usually held to pick out the strongest of those present. Many families on the worlds of the Advex-Mors system will train their sons from birth hoping that they will be granted the honour of joining the Chapter.
While physical strength is prized, the fighting styles of all aspirants is examined with those showing efficiency of actions are specially picked out. Displays of boisterousness and arrogance are likely to see the offending boy passed over for selection.
Those shortlisted are then taken back to Advex-Mors Primus (if it is efficient to do so) and dropped on the plains to the east of the hills in which Lastwatch Hold is found, and are told to make the near 4 day journey to the fortress monastery alone. Any who make the journey successfully are accepted as full aspirants.
Failed aspirant treatment:
If for some reason an aspirant cannot move on further in their journey to becoming a full battle-brother, they usually join the ranks of the Chapter serfs, though this is very rare as this situation almost never happens.
Role of Neophytes :
Like the vast majority of space marine Chapters, a neophyte’s first taste of combat is as part of scout squads in the 10th Company.
Process of becoming a Battle-Brother:
Upon the implantation of a neophyte’s Black Carapace they gain the ability to fully interface with a suit of power armour and are promoted to the role of a full battle-brother and are reassigned to a different company.
A neophyte receiving their power armour is done in a ceremony of celebration. In attendance is the neophyte and their previous scout squad, the sergeant who trained them, a chaplain, and the sergeant of the squad they have been assigned to. If they are available, a lieutenant or sometimes even the captain of the 10th company will attend if the newly elevated battle-brother has shown particular promise.
Reaction to Primaris:
The first Primaris marines were respectfully welcomed into the Dreadbringer. With their main base of operations located within Imperium Nihilus, the Chapter was not at full strength when the Indomitus Crusade arrived with what turned out to be much needed reinforcements. Being the pragmatic Chapter that they are, the Dreadbringers recognised the advantages this new breed of battle-brother brings, and the primaris marines were quickly integrated into the Chapter. There were some concerns that the remaining first-born members of the Chapter would be left behind, but this has ultimately not been the case, and the improvements and refining of the rubicon primaris procedure after the plague wars has meant that elevation to a primaris marine is available to any first-born marine who wishes it. Almost all of the high-ranking members of the Chapter have now undergone this procedure. As the Chapter maintains a large store of relic armours from the time of the great crusade and horus heresy, these suits have been modified to allow those of primaris physiology to wear them. This includes the Regalia of the Shattered Sceptre worn by the Dreadmaster.
There is one caveat to the Chapter's acceptance of primaris marines however. The first group of primaris marines given to the Chapter during the indomitus crusade, while respected as very capable and effective combatants, were not seen as true Dreadbringers. They had not joined the Chapter as aspirants, had not risen through the ranks from a neophyte to a true battle-brother, and had not known the Chapter's culture and traditions as they had known life as a space marine outside of the Chapter. For these reasons, none of the first batch of primaris marines were ever promoted beyond the rank of sergeant, and even those who made it to that rank are but a handful. This also means none were made members of the inner circle as their split loyalties to the Chapter and Belisarius Cawl could not be overlooked. Only a handful of these first marines remain, and new primaris marines who have followed the standard methods of joining and rising up in the Chapter do not bear this hidden restriction.




Doctrine
Chapter philosophy:
The Dreadbringers see themselves, and all space marines, as the vanguard of humanity against the terrors of the galaxy. Both the sword and shield of the people of the Imperium. To live and give your life in service of the Emperor and humanity is to live with honour. Any space marine who shows any hesitation in the execution of this duty is of the highest dishonour.
Highest values:
The Dreadbringers revere the qualities of the first legion of the great crusade, and of their father and Primarch, Lion El’Jonson. They are master strategists and while specialising in bringing complete annihilation to their foes, do so with the utmost efficiency. Efficiency in all things is prized within the Chapter as time, energy, and resources saved with efficiency can be spent on other endeavours.
Like all those of the Lion’s sons, the Dreadbringers are resolute in the face of any enemy, showing a level of discipline unmatched amongst astartes. No matter how many battle-brothers are deployed, the entire force acts in unison, and so the discipline is highly valued.
Both in and out of battle, Dreadbringers act with a chivalry and honour reminiscent of the old knight houses of Caliban.
Perception of the Emperor:
The Dreadbringers do not revere the Emperor as a god, and do not follow the “Imperial Creed”, instead believing in the Imperial Truth of the Great Crusade. To them the Emperor is a man, the greatest and most perfect of men, but still a man, not a god. Like most Unforgiven Chapters, the Dreadbringers venerate their primarch as much as they do the Emperor.
Views on death:
To die in service of humanity is to die with the utmost honour. Those battle-brothers who perish in the name of the Emperor and the Lion are regarded with great respect, and their name and deeds are recorded in the Chapter’s records. All Dreadbringers know that one day they will meet their end on the battlefield, however to do so recklessly and foolishly will earn little respect. Die in honourable service, not careless ineptitude.
Adherence to Codex Astartes:
While not disregarding the codex completely, it is certainly not followed to the letter. The codex is a useful tool for large organisational structure and for logistics, but in the eyes of the Dreadbringers, leaves a lot to be desired on the battlefield. To restrict one’s tactics is to invite failure, something the Dreadbringers will not accept.
Traditions:
The Dreadbringers employ many of the traditions and rituals of the old Dark Angels legion, though the details and specifics have changed over time as, while the Chapter maintains a large and detailed library of the traditions of the old legion, this knowledge has degraded over time.
Within the Chapter there are still some of the old orders of the old legion, as well as some newer ones, though because of the fewer numbers, there are nowhere near as many orders as there were during the Great Crusade.
Character and behaviour:
Generally, the Dreadbringers embody the traits of stoicism, seriousness, and efficiency. When there is a job to do, both in and out of combat situations, a Dreadbringer will devote themselves to its completion completely. Because of this strive for efficiency there is a need for unbreakable trust between battle-brothers. This is maintained by the intense training all battle-brothers go through, and is the responsibility of the Chapter’s Chaplains to monitor. Because of this, each Dreadbringer is incredibly trusting of each other Dreadbringer (especially when they share membership of one of the secret orders), however they are very slow to trust anybody outside of the Dreadbringers or other Unforgiven Chapters.
Despite, or perhaps because of the stoicism typical of the Chapter, a dry sense of humour is common amongst Dreadbringer battle-brothers, from the youngest neophytes all the way to the upper echelons. Allies fighting alongside the Dreadbringers who hear snippets of this sense of humour are likely to be confused or disturbed.

Fleet Description:
The Dreadbringer fleet is led by the Retribution Class Battleship Intolerant. The Intolerant served as the capital ship for the Dreadwing of the original Dark Angels legion, and was given to the Dreadbringers upon their founding. Mounted with a devastating annihilator cannon, a massive vortex weapon capable of deleting entire enemy fortresses from reality.
As well as the Intolerant, the Chapter fleet includes 3 Battle-Barges, 9 Strike Cruisers, and a large number of escort craft.
Multiple craft in the fleet, especially the older ships, are equipped with / contain archeotech devices originally belonging to the Dark Angels Legion. While incredibly useful, these are used sparingly and only in the direst of situations, lest word spread and the attention of the Inquisition is piqued.
Command structure
General description:
The general command structure of the Chapter follows the codex astartes. The Chapter is led by a Chapter Master (called the Dreadmaster), one Captain per company under whom serves two Lieutenants, with each squad led by a Sergeant. Outside of this structure are the first two companies, the Librarius, Reclusiam, Apothecarion, and Armourium. 
Standard role advancement:
In the Dreadbringers, the standard role advancement is from scout, to Devastator, to Assault, to Tactical marine. While this matches the recommendations of the Codex, the reason that Dreadbringers start in Devastator squads is that the destruction they wield is central to the combat philosophy of the entire Chapter, so an in-depth and practical knowledge of the purpose, tactics, and techniques of Devastators is crucial to every other role in the Chapter. Also note that despite accepting Primaris marines into their ranks, the Dreadbringers still use the pre-primaris terms for these squads, instead of their updated names of fire-support, close-support, and battleline.
Climbing the ranks:
Typically for space marine Chapters. Displays of intelligence, strategy, and organisation will lead a battle-brother down the path of command with a promotion to sergeant in their future.
Generally, Captains and Lieutenants are promoted from the ranks of the first company (Naufragia) Sergeants and Lieutenants, though this is not always the case. The main exception for this is the second company (Broken Claws) which exclusively promotes from within.
As is the case with all Unforgiven Chapters, promotion above the rank of sergeant, or into the Naufragia is dependent on whether the battle-brother can be trusted with the secrets of the Chapter and the legion, as promotion brings them closer to the Chapter’s Inner Circle.
Chapter Master’s role:
The chapter master of the Dreadbringers carries the title of “Dreadmaster”. Like all Unforgiven chapter masters, the Dreadbringer is also a member of the larger Inner Circle of all the Dark Angels successors, sharing the same, total knowledge of the legion’s past, and the secret of the destruction of Caliban and the existence of the Fallen.
Within the Chapter, the Dreadmaster is head of the internal Inner Circle and holds absolute authority within the Chapter. They are also charged, as is the Chapter as a whole, with the protection of the relics and knowledge previously protected by the Order of the Broken Claws. The Dreadmaster is the only one permitted full access to these, and thus is the person with the greatest knowledge of the Rangda and their xenocides in the entire Imperium.
Captains’ roles:
Dreadbringer Captains, like Captains of other Unforgiven Chapters, are known as “Masters” and command one of the 10 companies of the Chapter where they serve as the direct superiors of the Chapter’s Lieutenants. Each Master also holds an additional title reflecting their specialist duties.
Masters’ specialist duties:
Master of the Naufragia- 1st Company (Naufragia) Captain:
2nd in command of the chapter, and leads the specialist Naufragia company of terminator armoured veterans. They also serve as master of the keep, responsible for the chapter’s Fortress-Monastery Lastwatch Hold.
Master of the Broken Claws - 2nd Company (Broken Claws) Captain:
Leads the specialist fast-attack company, called the Broken Claws. Not known to any outside of the Inner Circle, the Master of the Broken Claws is head of the Chapter’s efforts to hunt members of the Fallen.
Master of the Arsenal - 3rd Company Captain:
Responsible for the proper issuing and retrieval of the Chapter’s arms and armour. They work closely with the Master of the Forge (Head Techmarine) to ensure these are maintained and accounted for.
Master of the Fleet - 4th Company Captain:
Oversees the Chapter’s fleet.
Master of the Marches - 5th Company:
Oversees the deployment of the Chapter's fighting strength. It is to his knowledge that the Chapter turns to when readying for war.
Master Victualler - 6th Company Captain:
Responsible for the non-armament provisions the Chapter requires to continue operations. As well as Serfs and Servitor management, they are also required to be a master in logistics.
Master of the Watch - 7th Company Captain:
Responsible for the system-wide defence and intelligence assessment of threats to the Chapter's homeworld, Advex-Mors Primus, and the whole Advex-Mors system.
Master of Condemnation - 8th Company Captain:
Acts as judge for internal disputes within the chapter, delivering verdicts and punishments as necessary.
Master of Relics - 9th Company Captain:
Oversees the Chapter’s many relics.
Master of Recruits and Reconnaissance - 10th Company Captain:
Oversees all neophytes from their first day in the Chapter, till their advancement to another company. This includes their training, psychological conditioning, and implants. They are also in charge of how these neophytes are deployed, usually in scouting and reconnaissance roles
Lieutenants:
Companies 3-10 each have 2 lieutenants that serve under the Company Captain. Each commands and is responsible for 50 battle brothers and the squads they make up. This makes up roughly half of the company.
The Naufragia and the Broken Claws (1st and 2nd Companies) do not have Lieutenants as they are not deployed in the same way as forces from the other Companies. As they are generally deployed in smaller, more specialised groups, Sargents fulfil this role. If needed, the Captain can appoint additional commanders to assist in the organisation and commandment of larger deployments.
Sergeants :
Sergeants are the lowest command rank in the chapter and lead individual squads on the battlefield. The specific battlefield role of the sergeant varies depending on the squad and their role on the battlefield, but each one is responsible for the battle-brothers that serve under them, be that ensuring all understand their mission, have the equipment they need, and carry out their objectives. They are also responsible for handling any problems that arise within their squad, escalating the issues to the Chaplains or Lieutenants if needed, however it is the preference of everyone that problems are handled "in squad".
Sergeants can be identified on the battlefield by a vertical red stripe on their helmet.
Veterans:
Veterans are the best and most experienced fighting members of a Company, consisting entirely of grizzled veterans of many battles.
Veterans are trusted with the most difficult, specialised, and important missions. To achieve this, Veterans are equipped with some of the best equipment the Chapter possesses. This usually consists of relic weapons and armour, some from as far back as the Horus Heresy.
Inner Circle:
Like with all Unforgiven Chapters, the Dreadbringers have an “Inner Circle” at the highest levels of the Chapter which consists of a number of officers who stand apart from the company organisation, and include the Chapter's Librarians and Interrogator-Chaplains. Companies are each led by a Master, who is part of the Inner Circle.
The Inner Circle's most important role is to guard the Chapter's darkest secret, that of the history and existence of the Fallen. Thus only the most trusted members of the Chapter are considered for membership within the Inner Circle, being observed and studied for many years beforehand until their loyalty is utterly assured. The Inner Circle hides its true face even from its own, taking great pains to hide the history of their Legion and only revealing the truth to its most trusted members.
To become a member of the Inner Circle, one must have served within the Naufragia (with the exception of the Broken Claws) and only upon their ascension to this elite force will they begin to learn the truth of their Chapter's past. However even after climbing to the Naufragia, there remain circles within circles and levels within levels, with each step accompanied by its own rituals of entry. Rising through the intricate ranks of the Inner Circle, a Dreadbringer will learn more and more of the truth of their Chapter's history, each secret revealed as the Chapter's trust in him grows.
Other:
Like both their parent legion and first founding chapter, there are many internal organisations and orders within the structure of the Dreadbringers. Impossible for an outsider to ascertain, even those within the chapter will never know the full extent of these orders. Only the Dreadmaster knows the complete internal structure of the chapter and its orders.




Specialist ranks
Librarian:
Dreadbringer Librarians fulfil many roles. Off the battlefield they are responsible for interstellar psychic communications, screening new neophytes for those of psychic potential, and maintaining the chapters records. In battle they utilise their abilities as powerful psykers. They are among the most knowledgeable of the Chapter's history and traditions.
On the battlefield, Librarian psychic powers often manifest in useful, and often destructive, ways. These can include anything from projecting energy bolts to erecting powerful protective fields. The more powerful Librarians tend to use their powers in more subtle ways, stepping out of time for a moment to anticipate their enemy's next move or redirecting bullets away from friendly targets.
To aid them in using these powers, and protecting themselves, Librarians wear psychic hoods. These devices incorporate psychically-nullifying crystals which provide defence against enemy psychic attacks. They are often armed with force weapons, which only Librarians and other psykers can use to full effect, channelling their powers through the weapon.
Dreadbringers' Librarians play a similar role to those of other Unforgiven chapters,aiding the Interrogator-Chaplains in extracting confessions from the Fallen by reducing their mental barriers and gathering the truth from the web of lies they speak.
Techmarine:
Techmarines also serve as mechanics and technicians, maintaining and repairing all the equipment of the chapter, from individual weapons, to entire ships. In addition to their engineering roles, Techmarines are often the pilots of Space Marine gunships and aircraft.
Because Techmarines pledge allegiance to both the Cult Mechanicus and their Chapter, they are never allowed to join the Inner Circle.
Chaplain:
Chaplains are the cultural teachers and leaders of the Chapter. They ensure that each battle-brother understands and embodies what it means to be a Dreadbringer.
As a secular chapter not believing in the Emperor as a god, Chaplains serve more as morale officers than as spiritual guides. Every battle-brother in the Chapter from the newest neophytes to the most experienced veteran will have a personal relationship with their company Chaplain, who’s advice and guidance can help them achieve greater feats in the name of the Dreadmaster, the Lion, and the Emperor.
The Chaplains are also responsible for ensuring that treachery and chaos taint is not present in the Chapter, sometimes taking extreme measures to ensure this.
Chaplains carry a Crozius Arcanum, serving as both a badge of office, and a potent power maul.
Interrogator Chaplain:
Sitting a level above regular Chaplains, Interrogator-Chaplains are members of the Inner Circle tasked with interrogating captured Fallen, either aiming to prise information, or rarely, a repentance. For each repentance gained by an Interrogator-Chaplain, they gain a black pearl for their rosarius.
Capturing a member of the Fallen alive is a rare event, so the most common prisoners interrogated by the Interrogator-Chaplains are not Fallen.
Apothecary:
The medically trained biologists of the chapter, Apothecaries act both as battlefield medics, and as general doctors and medical experts. This can include accompanying their battle-brothers into combat, monitoring recruits and neophytes for mutation or flaws in their gene-seed organs and implants, and for maintaining the Chapter’s stores of gene-seed.
Each has a diagnostor helmet that assists with checking health states, narthecium which allows to heal a space marine’s unique physiology, and a reductor which allows the extraction of gene-seed from the bodies of fallen battle-brothers.
Champion:
The greatest melee combatants of the company/chapter, Champions serve in the command squad of their Captain. In the case of the Chapter Champion they serve in the Dreadmaster’s Honour Guard.
Not an officer of rank, Champions are recognised for their immense skill in close combat, as well as their honour and dedication to the chapter. A Champion's primary role is to engage enemy warlords and champions in single combat, so that their commanding officer may conduct the wider battle free from interference. They also hold key roles in conducting the various rituals and ceremonies of the Chapter. When an honour duel takes place, such as those that take place when the Dreadbringers fight alongside the Space Wolves, it is usually a Champion who will represent the Dreadbringers.
Champions wield the highest quality armour and melee weapons available to the Company/Chapter, usually taking the form of a power sword, and sometimes a shield, however the exact weapon is ultimately up to the Champion themselves. Through the ages there have been champions that have chosen everything from axes to maces.
Ancient:
Ancients have been given the honour of carrying their Company banner into battle, and sometimes even the Chapter banner, inspiring those around them to fight harder and with more determination and zeal. It is the duty of the Ancient to never let his banner drop while he still draws breath. Losing his banner while still living is seen as a terrible and shameful dishonour, so to this end these individuals are invariably great warriors. On the battlefield they are the Chapter personified.
As befits this honour, Ancients are equipped with exquisite relic armour, adorned with many symbols and marks of the Chapter.
Honour Guard:
The retinue and bodyguards of the Dreadmaster. Within the Dreadmaster’s honour guard are the Chapter Champion and Chapter Ancient. Honour guards are two extremes, solemn and taciturn at peace but ferocious and unyielding in combat. They have achieved all awards and honours that a marine can, which puts them in a position of unrivalled knowledge. Often their experience and knowledge of warfare outstrips the Captains of a chapter, but to offer unsolicited advice would be to undermine the position of these captains. To this end, any advice given by an honour guard carries much weight within the command circle as well as among the rank and file. Even Dreadmasters have been known to seek advice from honour guards.
Honour guard weapons and equipment are the oldest and greatest the chapter has to offer. Most often this includes ancient and ornate artificer armour and weapons which have had hundreds of previous users, all of whom were great heroes. Some equipment used by Honour Guards even dates back to the time of the Great Crusade.

Chaplain:
No formal criteria is needed to be accepted into the Reclusiam, though it is usually those who show the most devotion to the Chapter’s culture that are chosen. Each aspiring Chaplain must be nominated by an existing Chaplain , and must gain the approval of at least 3 others before being formally accepted into the Reclusiam.
Interrogator Chaplain:
Chosen from the ranks of either long serving, or recently inducted Chaplains, those with the combination of extensive historical knowledge of the First Legion, as well as a skill for interrogation are the most likely to become Interrogator Chaplains.
Apothecary:
Any who show medical talent or an affinity for biological science.
Champion:
The most skilled and experienced melee combatant in their company. There is no formal method for choosing, and it is up to the Captain of the company to choose who will be that company’s Champion.
The Chapter champion is chosen from the existing Company Champions, being hand-picked by the Dreadmaster himself.
Ancient:
Like the role of Champion, the Ancient is hand picked by the Company Captain to carry the Company Banner. Chosen from the company’s veterans, it is usually the longest serving veteran who is chosen, though this is not always the case.
The Chapter Ancient is similarly chosen by the Dreadmaster, and is usually the longest serving veteran of the entire Chapter, though again, this is not always true.
Honour Guard:
Not a static formation, the Honour Guard is assembled as needed from the most experienced and skilled veterans present.


Strategy
Style of combat
The Dreadbringers specialise in the total annihilation of the enemy, and take to the field of battle with such an aim.
        Mass armoured assaults expose the weaknesses of the enemy, and overwhelming destruction at key points destroys them completely.
If an enemy is making use of technology such as advanced shielding or long range heavy weaponry that prevents the chapter from bringing their overwhelming destruction to bear, they may deploy a limited number of Vanguard Marines to sabotage and plant explosives to disrupt these defences, ideally doing so without the enemy realising their defences are compromised until it’s too late.
The Dreadbringers excel in the use of unconventional weapons, some from the Dark Age of Humanity, given to the original Dreadwing by the Lion and the Emperor.
        When the Dreadbringers are finished, there will be no enemy remaining, no remains of the enemy to recover, and no sign that the enemy was ever there save for the gaping holes left by the advanced weapons of the chapter.
Favoured weaponry
The weapons of the Dreadbringers are amongst the most destructive in the Imperium. Plasma, phosphex, vortex, melta, rad, volkite, and conversion beam weapons can all be found in the Dreadbringers arsenal.
        The Dreadbringers also utilise mass armoured assaults of Land Raiders, Spartan Assault Carriers, Mastodons, Whirlwinds, Vindicators, Arquitor Bombards and Fellglaives with advanced warp weaponry.
Strengths
Like the Dreadwing of old, the Dreadbringers are specialists in total purges and annihilation of the enemy. They are experts at the safe use of the more dangerous technologies of the Imperium.
They also excel at combating the more unconventional foes that humanity encounters. There is no enemy for which the Dreadbringers do not have a tool to fight against.
Weaknesses
The Dreadbringers are not best equipped, either in equipment or strategy, to take and hold targets. If an Imperial commander wishes for the fortress they are besieging, or the position they are assaulting to remain after the battle, they will be hoping the Dreadbringers are not the aid they receive.
They also do not work well in support roles with other forces of the Imperium, especially those who prefer to launch themselves into close quarters combat such as the Space Wolves or Blood Angels. Operating in close quarters to the weapons employed by the Dreadbringers requires extensive experience and knowledge in how to do so, which is rarely found outside of the Unforgiven or the Dreadbringers themselves.
